    For things without a barcode, like furniture, toys and chargers. RecallCheck compares your photo with product photos from US product recall notices since 2018. A second photo of the label, with the model number in focus, lets it check that number too.

    The first photo search downloads the image model and text reader (about 31 MB) once. Both run on your phone; the photo is not uploaded.

    How the check works

    Barcode. An exact match against barcodes printed in US recall records. Food recalls often list a barcode; household product recalls rarely do, so for those use Photo or Name.

    Photo. An image model turns your photo into numbers on your phone and finds recall photos that look alike. It shows possible matches and how to tell whether yours is included. It cannot confirm a match. A text reader also looks for a model number on the photo; if a recall notice names that number, the recall is shown first.

    No match means the item was not found in these records. It does not mean the item is safe.

    Data from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (openFDA food enforcement reports) and the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (Recalls API). RecallCheck is a student project and is not affiliated with either agency. Always read the official notice.
